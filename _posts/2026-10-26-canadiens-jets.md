Quand j'étais petit, mon père arrosait la cour en décembre. Une bande de deux-par-six, un boyau d'arrosage qui gelait à moitié dans ses mains, et le lendemain matin, une patinoire grande comme un mouchoir. Tous les petits gars du quartier venaient. On jouait jusqu'à la noirceur. Et à six heures, quand les mères criaient « Souper ! » par les fenêtres, tout le monde rentrait. Sauf un. Il y avait toujours un petit gars qui restait dans les buts, tout seul, à arrêter des rondelles imaginaires.

Dimanche, le Canadien a joué la Classique Héritage, dans un stade de football de Winnipeg. Dehors, comme dans la cour de mon père. Moi, mon patron n'a évidemment pas payé l'avion. J'ai regardé ça de ma cuisine, avec une tuque sur la tête par solidarité, et un deuxième bouilli sur le feu, parce que vendredi, je n'avais pas soulevé le couvercle et ça avait marché. On ne change pas une recette gagnante. On la recuit.

Vingt minutes, aucun but. Alexandre Carrier prend une punition pour obstruction à 16:14, et les Jets ne font rien avec. C'était comme les premières minutes sur la patinoire de la ruelle : tout le monde patine, personne ne sait où est le filet, et quelqu'un finit par perdre une mitaine.

J'ai gardé le couvercle fermé. Le calepin aussi. Le chat surveillait les deux.

À 7:19 de la deuxième, **Lane Hutson** ouvre la marque, sur des passes de Noah Dobson et de Cole Caufield. 1-0. Son troisième but de la saison. Hutson sur une patinoire extérieure, c'est le petit voisin que tout le monde veut dans son équipe parce qu'il ne tombe jamais.

Un défenseur qui compte deux matchs de suite, c'est tellement rare que j'ai failli ouvrir le calepin pour l'écrire. Je me suis retenu. Je l'ai écrit sur une serviette de table.

Alex Newhook prend une punition à 12:53 pour avoir porté son bâton trop haut. Les Jets n'en profitent pas. Caufield, lui, finit la soirée avec deux passes et aucun tir. Il a décidé de donner au lieu de recevoir. À son âge, moi, je ne savais même pas que c'était permis.

Et **Jacob Fowler**, lui, n'est pas rentré souper. **33 arrêts sur 34 tirs**, première étoile. C'était lui, le petit gars qui reste dans les buts quand tout le monde est parti.

When I was a boy, my father flooded the backyard every December. A two-by-six for boards, a garden hose half-frozen in his hands, and the next morning a rink the size of a handkerchief. Every kid in the neighbourhood came. We played until dark. And at six o'clock, when the mothers yelled "Supper!" out the windows, everybody went inside. Except one. There was always one kid who stayed in net, all alone, stopping imaginary pucks.

On Sunday, the Canadiens played the Heritage Classic, in a football stadium in Winnipeg. Outdoors, like my father's backyard. My editor, naturally, did not pay for the flight. I watched from my kitchen, wearing a toque in solidarity, with a second pot roast on the stove, because on Friday I had not lifted the lid and it had worked. You do not change a winning recipe. You reheat it.

### The first period, or the game of the lost puck

Twenty minutes, no goals. Alexandre Carrier went off for interference at 16:14, and Winnipeg did nothing with it. It was like the first few minutes on the backyard rink: everybody skates, nobody knows where the net is, and somebody eventually loses a mitten.

I kept the lid on. The notebook too. The cat kept an eye on both.

### The second, when the big kids show up

At 7:19 of the second, **Lane Hutson** opened the scoring, from Noah Dobson and Cole Caufield. 1–0. His third goal of the season. Hutson on an outdoor rink is the kid next door everyone wants on their team because he never falls down.

At 11:04, **Kaiden Guhle** made it 2–0, from Caufield and Juraj Slafkovský. Guhle scored in Chicago on Friday and scored again in Winnipeg on Sunday. A defenceman scoring in two straight games is so rare that I nearly opened the notebook to write it down. I held back. I wrote it on a napkin.

Alex Newhook took a high-sticking minor at 12:53. Winnipeg did not cash in. Caufield, for his part, finished the night with two assists and no shots. He decided it was better to give than to receive. At his age, I did not even know that was allowed.

### The third, or the goalie who would not come in for supper

Then Winnipeg started shooting. From everywhere. Mark Scheifele alone fired **seven shots** in the game. In all, the Jets peppered Montréal with **34 shots**, against **21** the other way. Newhook went back to the box at 5:08 for tripping. The roast was boiling, I was sweating under my toque, and the cat had his ears flat.

At 15:24, **Isak Rosen** finally beat Fowler, from Gabriel Vilardi and Haydn Fleury. 2–1. It stuck to the bottom, just as I had predicted. Only a little.

Then Josh Anderson went off at 18:28, and Skinner, who spent **58:25** in his crease, eventually gave up his spot for an extra attacker. I did not watch those last minutes. I listened to them with my eyes closed, the way you hear your mother yelling "Supper!" through the window.

And **Jacob Fowler** did not come in for supper. **33 saves on 34 shots**, first star. He was the kid who stays in net after everyone else has gone home.

### The crystal ball, head down (but not too far)

I had predicted **Jets 4, Canadiens 2**. Final: Canadiens 2, Jets 1. Second straight prediction in the ditch.

But look closely. I said the Canadiens would score two goals. They scored two. Exactly. My only problem is the other team. I have never claimed to be a Jets specialist. I am a Canadiens specialist, and on the Canadiens, I was right.

### What we bring home

- **Jacob Fowler**: 33 saves on 34, first star. Five wins in six starts this season and a **.924** save percentage. I dare not say it too loudly, in case he hears me and goes in for supper.
- **Lane Hutson**: the opening goal, second star, **21:12** of ice time.
- **Kaiden Guhle**: a goal, three blocked shots, **23:10**.
- **Cole Caufield**: two assists. The backyard playmaker.
- **Mike Matheson**: **25:37**, the most ice time on the team. He built the rink, flooded it and shovelled it.
- Winnipeg did not take a single penalty all game. Montréal took four. And we still won. I understand none of it, but I will sign for it.

### Last word

A 6-3-2 record, fourteen points, fifth in the Atlantic, and two wins in a row. One win makes me suspicious. Two make me paranoid. I checked the standings three times, turned off the burner and took off my toque.

On Tuesday, it is St. Louis. I am keeping the pot roast, but I am buying a new pot. You never know how long luck can simmer.

*— Gaston "Grumpy" Ronchon*
